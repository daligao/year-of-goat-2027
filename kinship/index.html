Why does Chinese have so many kinship terms?

English uses "aunt" for four different people. Chinese is precise: 伯母 (father's older brother's wife) ≠ 婶婶 (father's younger brother's wife) ≠ 姑姑 (father's sister) ≠ 姨妈 (mother's sister).

This matters because Confucian family ethics assign specific duties and forms of address to each relationship. Getting the title wrong signals that you don't know your place in the family hierarchy — a significant social misstep.

Key distinction: 堂 (táng) cousins share the father's surname (paternal side). 表 (biǎo) cousins are on the maternal side. Only 堂 relatives are considered "inside the family" (家里人) in traditional culture.

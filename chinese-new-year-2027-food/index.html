Chinese New Year 2027 Food

The classic dishes served for Chinese New Year 2027, and the symbolic meaning behind each one.

Classic Dishes & Their Meaning

🐟

Whole Fish

"Fish" (鱼, yu) sounds like "surplus." Served whole, often left partly uneaten, to symbolize abundance carrying into the new year.

🥟

Dumplings

Shaped like ancient gold and silver ingots — eating them is meant to invite wealth. A popular family-cooking tradition in northern China.

🌯

Spring Rolls

Their golden, cylindrical shape resembles bars of gold, making them a natural symbol of wealth and prosperity, especially in eastern China.

🍚

Nian Gao (Rice Cake)

"Nian gao" is a pun on "higher year" — eating it symbolizes rising fortune, growth, and progress with each passing year.

🍜

Longevity Noodles

Served in long, unbroken strands representing a long life — traditionally not cut during preparation or eating.

🍊

Tangerines & Oranges

Their round shape and golden color symbolize fullness and gold; the words for "tangerine" and "orange" also echo "luck" and "wealth" in Chinese.

Foods to Avoid

Cutting noodles short

Since longevity noodles symbolize a long life, cutting them — during cooking or eating — is thought to symbolically cut the wish short.

Plain congee on the first day

Historically associated with poverty, plain rice porridge is traditionally avoided on New Year's Day itself, saved instead for later in the festival period.

Foods tied to unlucky wordplay

Some households avoid dishes whose names sound like unlucky words in Chinese — the same wordplay logic that makes other dishes lucky can work in reverse.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the most important Chinese New Year dish?

Whole fish is often considered the centerpiece dish, since "fish" (鱼, yu) sounds like the word for "surplus" — the wish is to have abundance left over into the new year. It's traditionally served whole and often not entirely finished, symbolically leaving something behind for the future.

Why do people eat dumplings at Chinese New Year?

Dumplings are shaped like ancient Chinese gold and silver ingots, so eating them is meant to invite wealth in the coming year. Making dumplings as a family activity on New Year's Eve is also a common bonding tradition in northern China.

Is it true you shouldn't cut noodles?

Yes — longevity noodles (长寿面) are traditionally served uncut and eaten in long, unbroken strands, since cutting them is thought to symbolically cut short the long life they represent. Many cooks avoid chopping the noodles during preparation for the same reason.

What foods are considered unlucky at Chinese New Year?

Some households avoid foods associated with endings or bad luck — for example, foods pronounced similarly to words for "four" (which sounds like "death") are sometimes avoided, and plain congee or porridge is traditionally skipped on the first day since it was historically eaten only by the poor.
